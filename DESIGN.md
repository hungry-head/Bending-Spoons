# TripUp mobile design system

## Product character

TripUp follows the current Atlix TripUp mobile language shown in the supplied November 2026 references: a bright, compact utility interface with a cool mist canvas, deep navy actions, pale blue selected states, rounded white controls, real travel photography, and lightweight mint, sand, and violet category accents.

## Interaction model

- Thumb-first controls with 44pt minimum touch targets.
- Four global destinations—Home, Explore, Memories, and Account—sit around a fixed center Actions launcher in a fully rounded floating navigation pill inset from the screen edges. The five positions are ordered Home, Explore, Actions, Memories, Account. Actions uses a 44pt app-navy circle with a light plus; standalone floating Add and Ask AI pills are not used. Tapping it reveals a staggered horizontal row of contextual actions inside one connected, rounded, translucent navy-gradient glass tray above the navigation. Icons and labels are light on blue. The default set is Ask AI, Add activity, Add day, Add expense, and Scan receipt; Plan and Expenses show their relevant subsets, while Journal and Memories replace planning actions with Add photo and Write a memory. Selecting Ask AI animates an inline text composer above the action tray. Destination icons remain 22pt, while unselected icons retain a lighter stroke. The selected destination uses a vertical blue-to-surface gradient wash across its full navigation segment, without an icon capsule. Schedule, Finance, Profile, and the design-system catalog live beneath Account. In trip headers, adding a group member uses the light round control with a blue User Plus icon rather than the dark generic plus action. Packing and Documents align to the date row as compact translucent glass controls and collapse slowly to icon-only states while scrolling.
- Menu rows use the semantic list-item role (14/20 medium), with supporting details at 12/16 regular and group labels using the overline role. Bottom-sheet chrome titles use H4 (20/26 semibold); flow-specific titles inside full-height sheets may use H2 or H3 when they introduce the entire task.
- Itinerary cards place their schedule cue, either a time of day or an exact time, above the H5 event title. The single production card layout uses edge-to-edge place media that touches the card's left, top, and bottom edges, omits descriptive venue copy, and orders the category pill before compact participant avatars beneath the title with a small but visible separation. The fixed trailing lane keeps the overflow menu above the price, aligns the price to the top of the event title, and contains multi-digit fees within the card. Cards retain the category pill and comment action; prototype-only layout comparison controls are not shown.
- Timeline number markers are interactive visit controls: tapping a number replaces it with a success-colored check while the corresponding itinerary card remains visually unchanged. The control exposes checkbox semantics and a reversible accessibility label.
- A trip becomes its own focused map-backed bottom-sheet workspace. Overview is fixed first, while Plan and Expenses are also permanent tabs; Saved, Guides, and Journal can be added or removed through the fixed trailing three-bars customization control. The control stays pinned to the far edge without a container shape, while the label region scrolls horizontally when more than four tabs are visible. The customization sheet supports rearranging the remaining tabs while Overview stays first. The trip header and sticky tab row remain shared.
- Overview is a personalized cross-tab dashboard rather than a static introduction. Its final-day summary uses the same light card material as the rest of the product. It always summarizes Plan and Expenses, then conditionally adds Saved, Guides, and Journal highlights only when those tabs are present in the participant's configured navigation. As the trip approaches its end, Expenses is promoted to the first highlight so settling and outstanding balances are immediately visible; the remaining highlights retain the participant's configured order. Each compact highlight opens its source tab. Saved is populated with Lisbon restaurants and attractions, supports category filtering and save-state changes, and uses image-led list cards sized for quick planning rather than an empty placeholder.
- Design annotations are screen-scoped. Trip-detail annotations appear only on the Overview state; Saved, Plan, Expenses, Guides, Journal, analyses, menus, drawers, quick actions, member management, activity editing, and poll flows suppress unrelated annotation cards. Dedicated annotation states—such as tab customization or member invitation—replace rather than stack on top of the trip-level set.
- The Expenses tab opens with a light bordered summary card containing participant avatars, an add-expense action, the spend total, an inline editable budget directly beneath the amount, budget progress, a compact regular-weight remaining-budget badge, and a spending-analysis link. Per-day and per-person calculations are reserved for the detailed analysis screen. Below it, a two-state control switches between the five most recent expenses and recorded group balances. “View all expenses” opens a full-height, filterable ledger grouped into per-day buckets, with category icons, daily totals, a direct Analysis action, and the shared expense fixtures. Analysis places the day-by-day chart immediately after its total summary, before the compact settle-up card. The balance view summarizes simplified transfers and opens a full-height settle-up sheet with the current user’s payable transfer, read-only transfers between other members, and per-person balances; marking a transfer sent updates its status and progress. Expense rows use the shared heading/body hierarchy and neutral dividers. With no expense records, the list and balance controls are replaced by a centered receipt empty state and a primary Add expense action. The tab rail divider always spans the full sheet width; the fixed customization icon sits slightly beyond the label viewport so overflow tabs remain visibly discoverable.
- The wallet-plus action in the expense summary opens the Add expense sheet directly. The sheet uses H2 for its title and amount, H5 section labels, a body-style native name field, 44pt category and payer controls, a compact cover-mode selector, and a disabled primary action until both a positive amount and expense name are present.
- Add Expense supports four participant split states: equal, percentage, exact amount, and shares. Payers and split participants remain visible as a complete avatar set; compact plus/minus badges include or exclude them without removing the person from the layout. Percentage mode exposes an adjustable progress track and percentage per member. Exact mode uses euro amount fields. Shares mode uses compact minus/count/plus steppers. The selected participant set persists while switching modes.
- The populated Expenses screen uses a lightweight two-view tab line without count badges or filled segmented pills. The add-expense wallet is an outlined 44pt control with no plus badge. Recent expense fixtures vary by merchant, amount, member, date, and category so the list reads as distinct transactions.
- Trip Details treats its back action as an explicit return to Home rather than navigation-history back, keeping the exit destination predictable regardless of how the trip was opened.
- Add Activity supports Attractions, Food & Drink, Transport, Stay, Shopping, Entertainment, and Free. Selecting a category replaces the contextual option block rather than leaving irrelevant controls visible; Food & Drink retains saved restaurants and polling, while Stay exposes “Find a hotel from the list.” Member chips use 28pt avatars inside compact 40pt visual pills with expanded hit slop on their remove and add actions.
- Saved-place option cards derive their height from a responsive image ratio and three compact metadata rows rather than a fixed card height. All internal edges use the same 12pt inset. Availability appears as a translucent ribbon over the image, while travel time occupies a dedicated third row aligned to the trailing edge. Card width responds to the viewport between 220pt and 260pt.
- Sheets handle short creation flows; full screens handle planning and review.
- The reusable `BottomSheet` primitive provides animated entry, backdrop dismissal, configurable navigation insets, and interactive downward swipe-to-dismiss from anywhere on the sheet. Short drags snap back; committed drags move the sheet down while fading the backdrop. Drawers use the persistent drag handle and omit redundant visual close buttons unless a nested surface explicitly needs its own dismiss action.
- Form sheets use the shared `TextField` and large `Chip` variants for consistent inputs, selectable categories, disabled states, and 44pt minimum targets.
- Haptics acknowledge selection and primary actions.
- Press feedback uses opacity and subtle scale; structural navigation uses native transitions.
- Trip workspace tabs use a 210ms ease-out sliding indicator, slight selected-label emphasis, and a short content fade-and-rise transition. The final slot opens a tab-management sheet, where optional tabs use explicit add/remove controls and permanent tabs are visibly locked. Reduced-motion mode applies the selected state immediately.

## Foundations

- Tokens live in `src/design-system/theme/tokens.ts` and cover color, spacing, font sizes, line heights, weights, semantic typography, radii, elevation, and the four-column mobile grid.
- Core canvas is `#F5F8FC`, ink and primary actions use the navy family (`#102F55` and `#0D315A`), and link blue `#176FC1` is reserved for inline actions and selected utility states.
- System fonts are used for fast native rendering and Dynamic Type compatibility.
- Semantic text roles are the only source of text sizing across screens. The heading scale is H1 32/38, H2 28/34, H3 24/30, H4 20/26, H5 16/23, and H6 14/20, all semibold. Body is 14/20 light, supporting copy is 12/16 regular, overlines are 11/16 medium, list items are 14/20 medium, labels are 13/18 semibold, actions are 13/18 medium, pills are 13/18 regular, tabs are 14/20 regular, and captions are 12/16 medium. Native inputs and navigation labels consume these same roles.
- Action text is single-line by contract. Tertiary buttons use the 14/20 body size at medium weight and truncate with an ellipsis instead of wrapping.
- Primary buttons use fully rounded pill geometry across the app.
- Compact subscription and upgrade cards use the shared primary `Button` component rather than bespoke CTA styling. Their H5 title, supporting copy, and CTA follow a tight 10–12pt vertical rhythm without repeating the current plan in a separate status badge.
- Account identity is grouped in a dedicated bordered profile card. The trip-count badge sits inline with the member name, while the email remains supporting information directly beneath the name row.
- Compact Home prompts use the 14/20 medium list-item role rather than bold heading typography. They use 14–16pt card insets and preserve 44pt action targets without adding excess vertical space.
- Cards use 18–26pt corners; compact badges, filters, and bottom-navigation selections use pill geometry.
- Spacing follows a 4/8/12/16/24/32/48 scale.

## Component contract

Reusable primitives live in `src/design-system/components`, with a complete specimen catalog under Menu → Design system. The catalog separates Foundations and Components into accessible tabs. Feature screens compose these primitives and own domain-specific UI. Add variants to a primitive instead of re-creating its behavior in a feature. New controls must include pressed, disabled, loading, error, and accessible-label behavior where applicable. Visible interface icons come from Lucide.

Card variations are documented together in the Components catalog: standard itinerary, poll-generated itinerary, saved place, selected or poll-result place, and group member. They share semantic typography, 18pt corners, neutral borders, and 44pt action targets; selected and winning states add a stronger full border rather than changing the card structure.

## Web preview shell

The desktop web preview may include expressive tooling outside the phone frame. The web canvas uses a slow, organically moving and heavily blurred deep-purple gradient beneath its persistent arcade grid; moving color must read as ambient light rather than bounded shapes. The phone prototype is always anchored to the exact viewport center; outside-phone tools occupy independent absolute side rails and must never shift it. These supporting rails deliberately use reduced opacity, softened borders, and restrained glows so the centered phone remains the visual focus. The Chapters selector sits in the left side rail close to the phone, clearly inset below the phone’s top edge, uses an independent arcade-game visual language, and is intentionally exempt from TripUp’s mobile design system. It presents two expanded, left-aligned cards: `01 Open World Exploration`, selected by default with the description “Look through the app as you wish without a guided walkthrough,” and `02 Guided Experience`. Both top-level chapter headings are selectable. Guided Experience contains four additional independently clickable sub-options—Group Management, Collaborative Decision Making, Expense Tracking, and Expense Settling—visually nested on an indented connector tree beneath its parent. Group Management explicitly advertises three side quests and surfaces them below the annotation switch: adding trip-level members through the header, adding activity-level members through an itinerary card’s meatball menu, and comparing Guest versus Member behavior. Its primary guided path targets the add-person icon rather than opening the drawer automatically. After Ren is selected and invited, the drawer closes to Trip Details with a confirmation toast; one second later Ren enters the header avatar stack with a restrained scale/fade and becomes visible in the Manage Group `Guests` section. Selecting any option uses a restrained spring, transient cyan flash, and arrow nudge; reduced-motion mode applies both states immediately. The panel must remain web-only and must never leak typography, color, spacing, or layout changes into the phone screen unless explicitly requested.
Open World Exploration is annotation-only: it must never display guided prompts, coach marks, click targets, pulsing arrows, or prescribed next steps. Web-only annotations are enabled by default across the prototype and can be hidden or restored with the persistent `Annotations` switch in the right rail outside the phone. On Home, a `Trip at a glance` annotation sits beside the trip-summary card and explains: “When users arrive, the homepage brings together what’s happening now, today’s plan, and timely trip prompts in one clear view.” It is connected directly to the card with a short callout rule but never changes the phone UI.
Home also annotates the centered bottom-navigation action: “Actions reside here and are surfaced contextually across pages. On Home, the action is Ask AI.” Trip Details adds supporting annotations: the add-member/header action explains that header icons were updated to accommodate contextual Actions in the bottom navigation; Packing and Documents explain the redesign outcome: “Packing and Documents now transition into compact icons—keeping trip utilities accessible while reducing header clutter.”
Trip Details also annotates its configurable tab structure. The rationale identifies that travellers have different planning priorities and that a fixed structure creates navigation clutter; the design decision explains that participants can show, hide, and reorder optional tabs while core experiences remain consistent.
The trip-level Add Member flow has a web-only use-case annotation whose callout terminates directly at the contacts icon, stating that Ren already exists in contacts and required system permissions are granted. Search results require an explicit selection before the removal control and invitation CTA become available; removal returns Ren to the selectable result without clearing the query. The selected contact uses a compact iOS-style Guest switch. Because this use case occurs on the final trip day, selecting Guest assigns `Final day · 22 Nov` directly in the contact card instead of opening a date picker. Ren is absent from Manage Group until the invitation succeeds, then appears once in Guests with a distinct portrait. A successful invitation returns Home with a confirmation toast. Opening the QR invitation replaces the contact-search surface, hides the use-case annotation, and uses a reduced-motion-aware scale/fade transition in both directions.
When Add Member is entered from the Group Management guided flow, the contacts icon inside the search field receives the borderless pink pulse. The full 36pt contact control is interactive; selecting it fills the assumed contact query for Ren and removes that guide mark before selection continues. The guide then advances through Ren's result-level plus, the Guest switch, and the Invite friends CTA. Once Ren is selected, a right-rail annotation connected to the Guest switch explains that Guest access is temporary while leaving the switch off keeps the member for the entire trip, and directs users to the related side quest. After the invitation toast clears, the guide advances to the header avatar stack; selecting it opens Manage Group and scrolls Ren into view, completing the Group Management flow. The same drawer opened outside the guided flow has no pulse.
Guided Experience always behaves as a restart action. Selecting its chapter header from any route or nested flow clears guided progress and temporary drawer state, returns Home, and begins again at the first `See today’s plan` target. It uses a small, borderless, pink pulsing circle as its only in-phone target indicator. The pulse sits at the center of the target control, while the entire control—not only the circle—remains the valid guided hit area. While a target is active, pressing elsewhere in the phone opens an exit confirmation in the lower-right rail outside the prototype. “Yes, exit guided flow” returns to Open World Exploration and Home; “No, keep me here” dismisses the confirmation and preserves the current guided target. Annotations and guided target marks remain separate layers.
On Trip Details, the guided target moves to the fixed tab-customization icon. Selecting it opens tab customization and fades the page’s explanatory annotations so the interaction becomes the focus.
While Customize Tabs is open, three annotations explain the customization model. The Plan reorder arrow explains that users can rearrange, add, or remove tabs. Saved explains that Explore already exists in global navigation, so Saved replaces duplicated access with bookmarked trip-planning content. A branched callout connects Guides and Journal and explains that both remain optional rather than removed; the intent is to prioritize frequent trip-specific actions without sacrificing discoverability or personal choice. The guided pulse moves to the sheet’s close control, and the complete close control remains the valid guided hit area.
Collaborative Decision Making begins on Trip Details and guides the user through Plan → contextual Actions → Add Activity → Group unable to decide. Poll setup uses a vertical list of cohesive restaurant cards and requires at least two saved places, then returns to the activity editor rather than publishing immediately. The editor replaces `Add from your saved list` with a `Group poll` summary, identifies Participants, and explains that the poll will be sent to everyone in the activity. Adding the activity creates an empty itinerary poll attributed to `Ari (you)`, scrolls it into view, and surfaces the active poll in Overview. Casting a vote opens the live voting page; a participant can select, cast, and later recast their vote while seeing the current breakdown. Closing the poll surfaces an actionable group notification, and opening that notification shows the closed result with a `View activity` action that replaces the poll card with the winning itinerary activity. Each required action receives the same restrained pink pulse used by other guided flows, with reduced motion respected. The chapter rail also exposes a side quest for creating a poll outside an activity; Expense Tracking exposes receipt-scan and manual-exclusion side quests for excluding Ren and Nic from wine.

## Product architecture

- `src/app`: Expo Router routes only.
- `src/features`: future domain state, hooks, services, and feature compositions.
- `src/design-system`: app-local visual and interaction system.
- `src/data`: mock fixtures today; repositories and API adapters later.

## Screen language

- The active trip is Lisbon, 11–22 November, for 12 days. Home, Packing, Memories, Explore, Account, expenses, and all trip-entry routes must use this shared destination context.
- Home uses its own edge-to-edge scroll surface so the Lisbon map reaches the top of the simulated phone beneath the hardware island. The notification control retains compact glass treatment without a numeric badge, while weather becomes a fully opaque compact regular-weight control with a subtle white–navy–white gradient. Greeting, title, and weather form one tight vertical cluster. A single glass trip-summary card straddles the map boundary: its upper section contains the H5/supporting “Happening now” content, and its lower section pairs overlapping group avatars with a 46pt primary today-plan CTA that hugs its label instead of stretching. The following compact dismissible row reads “A TripUp widget right on your homescreen with Premium” in regular body weight and has neither badge nor chevron. Budget heads-up is presented as a contained rounded surface card before booking recommendations. The map-led hero is unique to Home; other phone screens keep their existing composition.
- The center launcher is context-aware: Home exposes only `Ask AI`; Trip Details always exposes `Ask AI`, `Activity`, `Add day`, `Expense`, and `Scan receipt` in the connected floating action tray.
- A single-action launcher hugs its action instead of stretching across the phone. Every launcher includes a transparent full-surface dismissal layer, so tapping outside the tray closes it without changing the underlying screen.
- Saved-place carousels use compact cards sized to reveal approximately 2.2 items within the phone viewport, making horizontal continuation immediately visible.
- Food & Drink and Attractions both surface populated saved-item carousels in Add Activity. Each supports single selection, a compact selected-item summary, and removal back to the carousel; restaurant polling remains exclusive to Food & Drink.
- Explore uses horizontally scrolling traveler guides and how-to cards with real photography and compact metadata.
- Memories leads with province progress and a journal empty state rather than a generic photo grid.
- Menu owns account, subscription, yearly recap, trip tools, settings, support, and the in-app design-system catalog.
- AI assistant is a focused violet-accent task surface with prompt chips, suggestion panels, usage meter, and a fixed composer.
- Packing uses a calm empty state and a native bottom sheet for category-aware item creation.
